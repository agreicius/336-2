Definition 1.19.9. Elgamal encryption.

Bob wishes to send messages to Alice over an insecure channel. The Elgamal cryptosystem accomplishes this as follows.
  1. Public.
    Alice publishes a large prime \(p\) and a primitive root \(g\) of \(\F_p\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Private.
    Alice chooses a random integer exponent \(a\text{,}\) and keeps this exponent private.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Public key.
    Alice computes \(A=g^a\in \F_p^*\) and publishes this value as her public key.
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Encryption.
    To encrypt a message \(m\in \F_p\text{,}\) Bob chooses a random integer \(k\text{,}\) computes the elements \(c_1=g^k\) and \(c_2=m\cdot A^k\) in \(\F_p\) , and sends the ciphertext \((c_1, c_2)\) to Alice.
    🔗
    🔗
  5. Decryption.
    To decrypt the message, Alice uses her private key \(a\) to compute
    \begin{gather*} m=c_1^{-a}c_2\text{.} \end{gather*}

    🔗
