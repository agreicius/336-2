Definition 1.19.10. RSA encryption.

Bob wishes to send messages to Alice over an insecure channel. The RSA cryptosystem accomplishes this as follows.
  1. Private.
    Alice chooses two distinct primes \(p\) and \(q\) that she keeps private.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Public key.
    Alice publishes her RSA modulus \(N=pq\) and an integer \(e\) such that \(\gcd(e,\phi(N))=1\text{.}\) The public key is thus the pair \((N,e)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Private key.
    Alice computes an integer \(d\) such that \(ed\equiv 1\pmod{\phi(N)}\text{.}\) She is able to do this efficiently, since she knows \(N=pq\text{,}\) can thus easily compute
    \begin{align} \phi(N) \amp =(p-1)(q-1)\text{,}\tag{1.19.2} \end{align}
    and then use the extended Euclidean algorithm to find a multiplicative inverse of \(e\) modulo \(\phi(N)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The integer \(d\) is Alice’s private key.
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Encryption.
    To encrypt a message \(m\in \Z/N\Z\text{,}\) Bob computes the ciphertext \(c=m^e\in \Z/N\Z\) and sends this to Alice.
    🔗
    🔗
  5. Decryption.
    To decrypt the message, Alice uses her private key \(d\) to compute
    \begin{gather*} m=c^d\in \Z/N\Z\text{.} \end{gather*}
    🔗
    🔗
