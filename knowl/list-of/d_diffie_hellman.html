Definition 1.19.2. Diffie-Hellman key exchange.

Alice and Bob wish to share a key over an insecure communication channel. The Diffie-Hellman key exchange accomplishes this as follows.
  1. Public.
    Alice and Bob agree publicly on a prime \(p\) and a primitive root \(g\) of \(\F_p\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Private.
    Alice chooses a random integer exponent \(a\text{,}\) and Bob chooses a random integer exponent \(b\text{.}\) These exponents are kept private.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Exchange.
    Alice computes \(A=g^a\in \F_p^*\) and sends this value to Bob; Bob computes \(B=g^b\in \F_p^*\) and sends this value to Alice.
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Shared key.
    Alice uses her private exponent \(a\) to compute \(B^a\in \F_p^*\text{;}\) and Bob uses his private exponent \(b\) to compute \(A^b\in \F_p^*\text{.}\) Since
    \begin{equation*} B^a=(g^b)^a=g^{ab}=(g^a)^b=A^b\text{,} \end{equation*}
    Alice and Bob are now in possession of a shared key
    \begin{equation*} k=g^{ab}\text{.} \end{equation*}

    🔗
