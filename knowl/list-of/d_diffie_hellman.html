Definition 1.19.2. Diffie-Hellman key exchange.

Alice and Bob wish to share a key over an insecure communication channel. The Diffie-Hellman key exchange accomplishes this as follows.
  1. Alice chooses a prime integer \(p\text{,}\) an element \(r\in \{1,\dots, p-1\}\text{.}\) She publishes the pair \((p,r)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Alice chooses a random integer \(a\) and computes \(A=r^a\boldmod p\text{,}\) and sends this to Bob.
    🔗
    Bob chooses a random integer \(b\) and computes \(B=r^b\boldmod p\text{,}\) and sends this to Alice.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Alice and Bob use their private exponents \(a\) and \(b\) to compute the shared key
    \begin{equation*} k=r^{ab}\boldmod p=B^a\boldmod p=A^b\boldmod p\text{.} \end{equation*}
