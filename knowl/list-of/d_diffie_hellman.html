Definition 1.19.2. Diffie-Hellman key exchange.

Alice and Bob wish to share a key over an insecure communication channel. The Diffie-Hellman key exchange accomplishes this as follows.
  1. Public.
    Alice and Bob choose publicly on a prime \(p\) and a primitive root \(g\) modulo \(p\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Private.
    Alice chooses a random integer exponent \(a\text{,}\) and Bob chooses a random integer exponent \(b\text{.}\) These exponents are kept private.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Exchange.
    Alice computes \(A=g^a\boldmod p\) and sends this value to Bob; Bob computes \(B=g^b\boldmod p\) and sends this value to Alice.
    🔗
    🔗
  4. Shared key.
    Alice and Bob use their private exponents \(a\) and \(b\) to compute the shared key
    \begin{equation*} k=r^{ab}\boldmod p=B^a\boldmod p=A^b\boldmod p\text{.} \end{equation*}
