Example 1.19.13. RSA: toy example.

Encode, and then decode the integer message \(m=50\) using the RSA public key \((N,e)=(77,7)\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Note that we have expressed things in terms of integers, as opposed to congruence classes. To encode, we must compute \(c=50^7\boldmod{77}\text{.}\) This will give us a chance to illustrate the idea behind fast modular exponentiation. Writing \(7=2^2+2^1+1\) we see that we can compute \(50^7\boldmod{77}\) as
\begin{align*} 50^7\amp \equiv 50\cdot (50^2)\cdot (50^2)^2\pmod{77}\\ \amp \equiv 50\cdot 2500\cdot 2500^2\pmod{77}\\ \amp \equiv 50\cdot 36\cdot 36^2\pmod{77}\\ \amp \equiv 50\cdot 36\cdot 64 \pmod{77} \\ \amp \equiv 29\cdot (-13)\pmod{77} \\ \amp \equiv 8\pmod{77} \text{.} \end{align*}
Thus the encoded ciphertext is \(c=8\text{.}\) Note that, knowing that \(77=7\cdot 11\text{,}\) we could have made our life even easier by computing \(50^7\boldmod 7=1\) and \(50^7\boldmod{11}=8\text{,}\) and then using the CRT to conclude \(50^7\boldmod{77}=8\text{.}\) However, in the RSA setting this technique should not be available to an encryprter, since they should not know the factorization of \(N\text{.}\)
🔗
To decode, we must find a multiplicateive inverse of \(e=7\) modulo
\begin{equation*} \phi(N)=6\cdot 10=60\text{.} \end{equation*}
The most direct way to do this is by applying the extended Euclidean algorithm to the pair \((7,60)\text{.}\) Since
\begin{align*} 60\amp= 8\cdot 7+4 \\ 7\amp= 1\cdot 4+3 \\ 4\amp= 1\cdot 3+1 \text{,} \end{align*}
we see that
\begin{equation*} 1=4-3=4-(7-4)=2(60-8\cdot 7)-7=-17\cdot 7+2\cdot 60\text{,} \end{equation*}
and thus \((-17)7\equiv 1\pmod{\phi(N)}\text{.}\) Thus we can choose any \(d\) in the congruence class \([-17]_{60}\) as our private key. To decode the ciphertext \(c=8\text{,}\) we must compute \(8^{-17}\pmod{77}\text{.}\) Sage can do this computation for us with no difficulties.
🔗
To compute this by hand, we could use fast modular exponentiation again, writing \(-17=-(2^{4}+1)\text{.}\) Alternatively, we can compute
\begin{align*} 8^{-17} \amp \equiv 1^{-17}\equiv 1\pmod 7\\ 8^{-17} \amp \equiv 8^{3}\pmod{11} \amp (-17\equiv 3 \pmod{10})\\ \amp \equiv 6\pmod{11}\text{,} \end{align*}
and conclude by the CRT that \(8^{-17}\equiv 50\pmod{77}\text{.}\) Thus
\begin{equation*} c^{-17}\boldmod{77}=50\text{,} \end{equation*}
as expected.
